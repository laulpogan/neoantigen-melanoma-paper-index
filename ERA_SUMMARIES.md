## Era I — In-vitro sensitization, LAK cells, autologous tumor (1988-1995)

The opening era of clinical adoptive immunotherapy in melanoma. Lymphokine-activated killer (LAK) cells, in-vitro-sensitized lymphocytes against autologous tumor lysate, and high-dose IL-2 became the first regimens to produce objective responses in metastatic melanoma. The mechanism — what specificities the products contained — was unidentified at the time. This is the era Theodore practiced in. The Rosenberg / Lotze / Mazumder protocols of this period are direct ancestors of every modern TIL therapy.

**Open-access:** 24 (12%)  
**Content mix:** 104 human research · 29 preclinical mouse · 21 reviews

## Era II — Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes + IL-2 (1988-2000)

TIL therapy emerges as a distinct approach. Tumor-derived T cells, expanded ex vivo with IL-2 and reinfused, produce objective responses where bulk peripheral lymphocytes did not. The seminal Rosenberg group NEJM and Science papers establish the TIL paradigm; Hwu, Topalian, Yannelli build out the protocol. Smaller corpus (n=46) because the era is short and contained.

## Era III — Defined-antigen vaccines (1995-2005)

MART-1, gp100, tyrosinase, and MAGE-A family antigens are characterized as melanoma-shared tumor antigens. The field shifts toward defined peptide vaccines — Romero/Cerottini at Lausanne, Yee/Riddell at Fred Hutchinson, and the Rosenberg group at NCI all run trials. Some objective responses are achieved, but central tolerance to these self-antigens limits the magnitude of T-cell responses. The failure mode of this era — high-affinity T cells against shared antigens are largely deleted in the thymus — is what motivates the eventual shift to neoantigens.

## Era IV — Adoptive cell transfer + lymphodepletion (2002-2015)

The Rosenberg group introduces lymphodepleting conditioning (cyclophosphamide + fludarabine, sometimes plus TBI) before TIL infusion. Response rates climb to 50%+ in selected metastatic melanoma patients, with sustained complete responses past five years. This era proves that adoptive T-cell therapy works clinically — the question is no longer whether, but how to standardize it and identify who responds. The 'active ingredient' (the T-cell specificity that drives response) remains uncharacterized.

## Era V — Neoantigen discovery (2013-2020)

The conceptual revolution. Whole-exome sequencing of tumors reveals patient-unique somatic mutations that produce non-self peptides — neoantigens — that T cells can recognize. The Snyder, Rizvi, and Schreiber groups demonstrate that neoantigens predict checkpoint-inhibitor response. The Tran/Robbins/Rosenberg group shows that successful TIL therapy is mediated by the subset of T cells reactive against patient-specific neoantigens. This is the mechanistic insight that retroactively explains *why* IVS and TIL ever worked.

## Era VI — Personalized neoantigen vaccines (2017-2026)

Two pivotal 2017 papers — Ott/Wu's NeoVax peptide vaccine (*Nature*) and Sahin/BioNTech's mRNA mutanome vaccine (*Nature*) — demonstrate that personalized neoantigen vaccines can prime tumor-reactive T cells in melanoma patients. Subsequent KEYNOTE-942 (mRNA-4157 + pembrolizumab) reduces recurrence in adjuvant melanoma by 44%. BioNTech autogene cevumeran fails in metastatic melanoma. The lesson: vaccine-only is insufficient when disease burden is high; combination strategies are needed.

## Era VII — Modern TIL clinical era — lifileucel approval (2018-2026)

Lifileucel (Iovance) becomes the first FDA-approved cellular therapy for any solid tumor (Feb 2024). The C-144-01 pivotal trial reports 31.5% ORR and 36.5-month median DOR in checkpoint-refractory metastatic melanoma. The regulatory pathway, payer landscape, and clinical infrastructure for autologous TIL therapy are now established. The unmet need that remains — higher complete-response rates and broader patient access — is exactly where Theodore's program targets.
